## Key Definitions (Strict)

### Subtask
A **subtask** is a goal-oriented, executable middle-level objective.
It is NOT a single atomic action (e.g., `pick`, `navigate`), but it MUST be concrete enough to be grounded to predicates.

❌ Invalid subtasks:
- pick_plate
- retrieve_object
- search_room

✅ Valid subtasks:
- place_plate_on_table
- move_plate_from_table_to_counter
- put_dirty_dishes_into_sink

---

## Domain Predicate Requirement (VERY IMPORTANT)

- Every `Subtask goal` in **Subgoal Grounding** MUST be expressed using **predicates defined in the domain file**.
- You MUST NOT invent new predicates.
- If the exact predicate names are unknown at revision time, **leave them as placeholder predicates**, but ensure they follow valid PDDL predicate structure.

Example placeholder:
    '(predicate_name arg1 arg2)'

This constraint MUST be explicitly respected for every subtask.

---

## Granularity and Execution Constraints

### Granularity
- Do NOT collapse multiple distinct goals into one subtask.
- Do NOT fragment subtasks into navigation-level or motor-level steps.
- The number of subtasks should remain **close to the original_cot**, unless evaluator feedback explicitly requires restructuring.

### Single-arm Robot Constraints
- **Retrieving an object is NOT a complete subtask.**
- A subtask must represent a **complete functional goal**, including the *use* or *placement* of the object.
- The robot can only hold **one object at a time**.
- Do NOT pick up two objects consecutively without an intermediate placement.
- If such violations exist in the original_cot, you MUST fix them.

---

## Task Decomposition Rules

1. **Actionable and Executable**
   Each subtask must correspond to something the robot can actually achieve in the environment.

2. **Internal Feasibility**
   Subtasks must be ordered so that all preconditions are satisfied.

3. **Integrity**
   Subtasks like `pick`, `grab`, or `retrieve` alone are incomplete and must be revised into full goals.
